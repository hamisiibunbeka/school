Part 1

The one rule: every transaction is recorded twice

Every movement of money touches at least two accounts. One side is recorded as a debit (Dr) and the other as a credit (Cr), and the two sides are always equal. This is called double entry. It keeps one equation true at every moment:

Assets=Liabilities+Equity+(Income − Expenses)

Debit and credit are not "plus" and "minus". They are simply the left and right columns. Whether a debit increases or decreases an account depends on the account's class:

Assets and Expenses grow with a Debit. Liabilities, Equity and Income grow with a Credit.

This is why the journal screen will not let you submit until Difference shows 0.00: an entry whose two sides differ would break the equation.

Two statementsThe Balance Sheet is a photo of one day: what the school owns and owes. The Income Statement is a video of a period: what was earned and spent between two dates. At year end, the surplus from the Income Statement moves into Equity.

Part 3

Worked examples

Real school transactions written as journal lines. In each one, the debits equal the credits.

1 · Term fees are billed

Fees of TZS 950,000 are invoiced to a Form One student.

AccountDrCr
1300 Student Fees Receivable950,000
4100 Tuition Fees950,000

The parent now owes money (asset up = Dr) and the school has earned it (income up = Cr), even though no cash has moved yet.

2 · The parent pays by M-Pesa

TZS 500,000 arrives in the mobile-money float.

AccountDrCr
1250 Mobile Money Float500,000
1300 Student Fees Receivable500,000

Cash goes up (Dr) and the amount owed goes down (Cr). Income is not touched again: it was earned when billed.

3 · Electricity bill paid from CRDB

A payment voucher of TZS 320,000 is approved and paid.

AccountDrCr
6xxx Electricity & Water320,000
1200 CRDB Bank – Operations320,000

The expense goes up (Dr) and the bank goes down (Cr).

4 · Stationery bought on credit

A purchase order for TZS 1,200,000 is received; the supplier will be paid later.

AccountDrCr
1400 Inventory – Stores1,200,000
2100 Accounts Payable – Suppliers1,200,000

Stock goes up (Dr) and the school owes the supplier (liability up = Cr). When stock is issued to a class, it moves from Inventory to an expense.

5 · Payroll is approved

Gross pay 2,000,000; PAYE 150,000 and employee NSSF 200,000 are deducted.

AccountDrCr
Salaries expense2,000,000
PAYE payable150,000
NSSF payable200,000
Salaries payable (net)1,650,000

The full cost is an expense. What is owed to TRA, NSSF and the staff sits in liabilities until it is paid. Employer contributions add a further expense and liability.

6 · Salaries are paid

The net pay leaves the bank.

AccountDrCr
Salaries payable1,650,000
1210 NMB Bank1,650,000

The liability is cleared (Dr) and the bank goes down (Cr). The expense was already recorded at approval.

For developersThe system writes all six of these automatically from Fees, Expenses, Purchase Orders and Payroll. Staff only write journals by hand for corrections, opening balances, transfers between bank accounts and depreciation.

Part 4

The "New ledger account" form, field by field

Open it from Accounting › Chart of Accounts › New account. The numbers match the arrows.

1

Account code (required)

Means
A short number that gives the account its place. The first digit is its class: 1 assets, 2 liabilities, 3 equity, 4 income, 5 direct costs, 6 operating expenses.
System
Must be unique in the school. Letters, digits, dots and dashes only.
Teach
Leave gaps (1200, 1210, 1220) so new accounts fit in order. Never reuse an old code for something else.
2

Account name (required)

Means
The words people will see on every report.
Teach
Be specific: "NMB Bank – Fees Collection", not "Bank 2". If two accounts sound alike, staff will post to the wrong one.
3

Class (required)

Means
Asset, liability, equity & funds, income or expense. It decides which statement the account appears on.
System
Cannot be changed once the account has postings.
Teach
Ask: "Do we own it, owe it, earn it or spend it?"
4

Sub-type (optional, but important)

Means
A finer label the software reads to place the account in reports and dropdowns. See the sub-type table.
System
Only cash, bank and mobile-money accounts appear in "Deposit account", "Paid from" and payment dropdowns. Sub-types also split the balance sheet into current and non-current, and the income statement into direct and operating costs.
Teach
A new bank account that does not appear when collecting fees almost always has the wrong sub-type.
5

Parent account

Means
Places the account under a group so reports can subtotal (1000 Assets → 1200 CRDB Bank).
System
The parent must be the same class, cannot be the account itself or one of its children, and must have no postings of its own.
Teach
Parents are folders; money goes into the files inside them.
6

Normal balance

Means
The side the account usually sits on: Debit for assets and expenses, Credit for the rest. It is filled in from the class.
System
Cannot be changed once the account has postings. Balances in the list are shown on this side.
Teach
The one common exception is a contra account such as Accumulated Depreciation: an asset that sits on the Credit side because it reduces the value of equipment.
7

Description

Means
A note for people.
Teach
Write when to use it: "Termly NECTA exam fees only" or "Diesel for school buses".
8

Postable (accepts journal lines)

Means
Ticked: a real account that money can be posted to. Unticked: a header used only to group and subtotal.
System
Headers never appear in posting dropdowns. An account with postings must stay postable.
Teach
Untick only for group rows such as "6000 Operating Expenses".
9

Control account (sub-ledger only)

Means
The balance is the total of detailed records kept elsewhere in the system. Here: 1300 Student Fees Receivable (every student's statement) and 2100 Accounts Payable – Suppliers (every supplier's balance).
System
Manual journals cannot post to a control account, except journals of type Opening or Adjustment.
Teach
If someone posts to 1300 by hand, the ledger no longer equals the sum of the students' statements, and nobody can reconcile them. Fix fees in Fees, and supplier bills in Purchase Orders.
10

Active

Means
Untick to retire an account.
System
Retired accounts keep their history and stay in reports but disappear from dropdowns. Accounts are never deleted once used.

Part 6

Reading the chart of accounts list

  1. 1Sub-type: how reports treat the account.
  2. 2Normal: the account's usual side, Dr or Cr.
  3. 3Flags: Header (group row), Postable (real account) or Control (sub-ledger).
  4. 4Balance, shown on the normal side as a positive number. Red in brackets means the balance has crossed to the wrong side.
  5. 5A control account. Post to it only through Fees or Purchase Orders.

The cards above the list

  • Accounts, Postable, Control accounts, Inactive count the accounts by type. Accounts minus postable = the number of header rows.
  • Assets, Liabilities, Equity & Funds, Income, Expenses are live totals from posted journals only. Drafts and journals awaiting approval are not included.
  • Sanity check: Assets should equal Liabilities + Equity + (Income − Expenses).

When a balance shows red in brackets

An asset with a credit balance, or a liability with a debit balance, almost always means something was posted to the wrong account. The usual cases in a school:

  • A bank account in red: more has been paid out of it than ever went in. Usually receipts were deposited to a different account (another bank or the M-Pesa float) while payments came out of this one. Check where fees were deposited. If the money really moved between accounts, record a transfer journal: Dr the bank that received, Cr the bank it came from.
  • Staff Advances & Loans in red: more has been recovered from salaries than was advanced. Look for a recovery deduction with no matching advance.
  • Student Fees Receivable in red: parents have paid more than was invoiced (prepaid fees). Check that the term's invoices were generated.

Investigate red balances the same week. They only get harder to trace.

Part 8

Setting up a new school's accounts

New schools start with a ready Tanzanian school chart of accounts. Adjust it before any money is recorded.

  1. Rename the bank accounts to the school's real banks and add one account per real bank account or M-Pesa till. Give each the sub-type bank, cash or mobile_money.
  2. Check the income accounts (4000s) match how the school wants to see fees: tuition, boarding, meals, exams, transport. Then link each fee category to its income account (Fees & Billing › Fee Categories).
  3. Add expense accounts the school actually uses. Mark teaching costs as direct_cost and running costs as operating_expense.
  4. Check payroll: each salary component points to a GL account, and the statutory rules point to the PAYE, NSSF, WCF, SDL and HESLB payable accounts.
  5. Open the fiscal year (Accounting › Fiscal Years).
  6. Enter opening balances with one Opening journal dated the first day of the year: bank and cash balances, fees owed, supplier balances, fixed assets, and the difference to Opening Balance Equity. A second user approves it.
  7. Run the Trial Balance. Debits must equal credits, and the bank balances should match the bank statements.

Part 9

Rules to drill, and what to do when the system says no

  1. Debits must equal credits. The system will not post an unbalanced entry.
  2. Never post to headers or control accounts by hand. Use Fees, Payroll or Purchase Orders.
  3. Posted is permanent. To fix a mistake, reverse the journal and post a correct one. The history stays visible to auditors.
  4. Two people for every manual entry. One prepares, a different user approves.
  5. Closed years are locked. Nothing can be posted into a closed fiscal year.
  6. Red brackets get investigated the same week.
  7. Pick the most specific account. A growing "Other expenses" hides where money goes.

Part 10

Glossary

Debit (Dr)
The left column of an entry. Increases assets and expenses.
Credit (Cr)
The right column. Increases liabilities, equity and income.
Journal
One balanced entry with two or more lines. Numbered JV-…
Ledger
The running list of every posting to one account, with its balance.
Chart of accounts
The full, numbered list of accounts the school uses.
Trial balance
Every account's balance on one page. Total debits must equal total credits.
Receivable
Money owed to the school, such as unpaid fees.
Payable
Money the school owes, such as supplier bills or PAYE.
Control account
A ledger account whose balance is the total of a detailed sub-ledger.
Sub-ledger
The detail behind a control account: student statements, supplier balances.
Header account
A non-postable grouping row that subtotals its children.
Contra account
An account that reduces another, so it sits on the opposite side (Accumulated Depreciation).
Accrual
Recording income when earned and expenses when incurred, not when cash moves. Billing fees before they are paid is an accrual.
Reversal
A new journal that exactly cancels an earlier one. Used instead of deleting.
Maker–checker
One person prepares, a different person approves.
Fiscal year close
Locking a finished year so its figures can no longer change.

Part 11

Self-check for training sessions

Ask the group first, then open the answer.

1. A parent pays TZS 300,000 cash at the bursar's office. Which account is debited?

Cash on Hand (1100). Cash is an asset and it went up. The credit goes to 1300 Student Fees Receivable, because the parent now owes less.

2. Why can't the bursar type a journal that debits 1300 Student Fees Receivable?

1300 is a control account. Its balance must always equal the sum of the student statements, which only the Fees module maintains. Billing is done by generating or issuing invoices.

3. The school opens a new Equity Bank account. What three settings make it usable for fee collection?

Class Asset, sub-type bank, and Postable and Active ticked.

4. A journal was posted to the wrong expense account last week. How do we fix it?

Reverse the journal and post a correct one (or post a correcting journal moving the amount). Posted journals are never edited or deleted.

5. CRDB Bank shows a red bracketed balance. What does it mean and where do we look first?

The bank account is on the credit side: more was paid out than recorded coming in. Check whether fee receipts were deposited to a different account, and compare with the CRDB bank statement.

6. Is Accumulated Depreciation an asset or a liability, and what is its normal balance?

An asset (a contra-asset) with a Credit normal balance. It reduces the value of equipment and vehicles.
